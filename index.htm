New York, NY Systems Research Writing
Abstract

Expertise holds inside a domain and fails predictably at its edge, where a shared surface hides a real difference underneath. The same failure shows up in a senior engineer misreading an unfamiliar system, a router in a mixture-of-experts model picking the wrong specialist, and a protocol asked to do a job that belongs to a different layer entirely.

What follows works that boundary from four directions: formal research, applied infrastructure, criticism, and teaching.

§1

Where expertise fails

A formal argument that domain expertise is a boundary phenomenon. It's reliable inside a domain and wrong, confidently and systematically, right at its edge, because structural similarity masks a real difference in what's actually happening underneath. The paper carries the argument into AI routing architectures: mixture-of-experts systems, multi-agent orchestration, RAG pipelines. The failure isn't missing capability. It's a system that sounds right while routing to the wrong specialist, or to no specialist at all.

Cited under its own logic. "Twilight of the MCP Idols" leans on this paper's routing argument mid-essay, in the middle of making an entirely different case about protocol design. Same failure mode, one register down.

§2

Built, not just argued

AIR-Kit is a local, offline incident-response engine. When a breach cuts off cloud AI, or a vendor can't tell attacker from defender and locks everyone out, AIR-Kit keeps running on your own hardware. It reads your existing telemetry, SIEM, EDR, identity, builds an evidence graph, and recommends containment with a full audit trail.

Detection is not understanding. That's the tool's own tagline, and it's the same gap the routing paper formalizes. Telemetry tells you what pattern fired. It doesn't tell you what actually happened. AIR-Kit is built for the boundary between the two.
